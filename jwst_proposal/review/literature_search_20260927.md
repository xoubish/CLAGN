# Literature and citation review — 27 September 2026

The proposal now has 50 references, including 14 papers added in this review.
Citations in the text and figure captions are numbered in order of first use.
The new bibliography entries link to their published DOIs. The five core pages,
figure sizes, sample membership, observing configuration and time request are
unchanged. Hemmati et al. (2026) was added at the user's request beside the
variability-manifold description.

## Search scope and verification

Searches covered changing-look discovery and selection, differences in spectral
criteria, infrared echoes, dust response functions, sublimation and replenishment,
silicate diagnostics, infrared luminosity and covering-factor estimates, empirical
host decomposition, and recent JWST spectral analyses. Searches also covered
2025–2026 work. The most relevant recent additions are Wang et al. (2026) and
Hermosa Muñoz et al. (2025); the existing Landt et al. (2026), González-Martín
et al. (2025), and Reyes-Amador et al. (2025) references remain appropriate.

New claims and citations were checked against the authors' papers or abstracts
on arXiv, publisher articles, and author-institution repositories. Publication
metadata were checked against the DOI records and published/institutional
bibliographic records where accessible. Some publisher pages could not be
fetched; author manuscripts and institutional copies supplied the scientific
content in those cases. This is a targeted literature search, not an exhaustive
systematic review or an independent reproduction of the cited results.

## Existing citations checked or placed more precisely

- **Zeltyn et al. (2024), now ref. 33:** Section II.6.2 discusses extremely
  variable sources whose broad lines remain visible, observational selection
  effects, and possible later transitions. This directly supports selection that
  does not depend only on a formal changing-look label. It is now cited beside
  the discussion of differing criteria as well as in the catalogue list.
  [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.01933v2).
- **Landt et al. (2026), now ref. 20:** the Mrk 817 temperature response supports
  the heating/cooling interpretation at an approximately fixed dust boundary.
  Its near-infrared decomposition also supports the general hot-dust motivation,
  without claiming a temperature light curve from the supplied SPHEREx spectra.
  [Primary abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.21395).
- **González-Martín et al. (2025), now ref. 28:** the paper explicitly separates
  point-like and extended MRS emission and identifies failures of current dust
  models. Those are the two uses retained in the proposal.
  [Primary abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01103).
- **Reyes-Amador et al. (2025), now ref. 43:** the explored distributions and
  compositions alter continua and both silicate features, supporting the proposed
  composition/grain-size uncertainty treatment.
  [Primary abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23981).
- Existing dust-radius, infrared-echo, optical-property and instrument references
  were retained; their previous bibliographic checks are distinguished from this
  review's new checks in `reference_audit.csv`.

## Deliberate boundaries

The proposed rising/fading offset, sample sensitivity calculation, specific
target histories, and ETC values remain this programme's calculations or
measurements. Added citations do not turn them into published results. The
equilibrium comparison and the delayed fixed-distribution comparison remain
distinct. No paper was cited as proof that a single MIRI epoch uniquely determines
dust evolution.

Additional studies considered included Almeyda et al. (2017), whose response
modelling is developed further in the included 2020 paper, and recent
object-specific proposals linking changing-look behaviour to torus structure.
They were not added simply to increase the reference count or to imply direct
evidence for the proposed warm spectral signal.

The [Cycle 6 PDF instructions](https://jwst-docs.stsci.edu/jwst-opportunities-and-policies/jwst-call-for-proposals-for-cycle-6/jwst-preparing-the-proposal-pdf-attachment)
allow five core pages for Medium GO proposals and do not limit the References
section. This version remains seven pages overall.
