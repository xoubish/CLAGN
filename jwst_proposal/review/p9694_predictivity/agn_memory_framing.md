# Put AGN history at the centre of the experiment

Update: this hierarchy has now been adopted in the proposal opening and analysis,
with the memory schematic as Figure 2 and the dust-evolution illustration as
Figure 3. The quantitative feasibility limitations below still apply.

Suggested lead question: **Does an AGN's infrared spectrum measure its current
power, or retain a measurable imprint of its recent accretion history?**

Suggested opening:

> Large luminosity changes in active galactic nuclei provide a timed experiment
> in how black-hole activity is recorded by the surrounding material. Optical
> light curves and spectra date the changing nuclear state, while infrared
> emission samples its delayed response. We will combine these histories with
> SPHEREx and MIRI spectroscopy to test whether rising and fading AGNs retain
> different spectral signatures at comparable present-day nuclear luminosity.
> The aim is to determine when an infrared spectrum traces current accretion
> power and when earlier activity must be included in its interpretation.

The sequence of science questions should be:

1. **Is recent history needed to interpret the present AGN spectrum?** Compare
   nuclear infrared luminosity and shape between rising/fading sources while
   accounting for current luminosity, host emission, extinction and geometry.
2. **How does the historical imprint depend on stage and elapsed time?** Use
   the dated light curves and spectra to relate hot/warm emission to rise,
   decline or recovery and to estimated response times. The ensemble samples
   different stages; one MIRI visit is not direct temporal monitoring of each
   object's warm spectrum.
3. **What response explains the memory?** Test whether delayed heating/cooling
   suffices or whether changes in the emitting material are needed. The paired
   silicate features help constrain this interpretation. Dust evolution is a
   possible outcome, not the sole criterion for programme success.

Operationally, the first comparison is between a current-luminosity equilibrium
description and a history-dependent response. Both fixed and evolving dust
families belong to the latter. A residual relative to a delayed fixed model
(the current proposal's delta_warm) tests **additional evolution**, not memory
in general. It should therefore become a secondary diagnostic if this framing
is adopted; simply relabelling that residual would not change what it tests.

The AGN payoff is a test of how recent accretion history affects infrared
estimates of nuclear luminosity and state. It is not a promise to identify the
accretion trigger, recover a unique unobserved light curve, or directly measure
an accretion-disc instability from one MIRI spectrum.

The P9694 audit demonstrates a conditional mechanism degeneracy. It does not
yet forecast the power of this revised ensemble experiment. That requires a
comparison of equilibrium and history-dependent predictions, with current
luminosity and nuisance parameters treated consistently, plus a sample-level
assessment of the detectable history dependence. The current Figure 2 is a
secondary mechanism illustration; its selected curve differences alone do
not supply that assessment.

This note is a proposed scientific reframing, not an already validated new
primary diagnostic or a rewrite of the submitted-format proposal.
