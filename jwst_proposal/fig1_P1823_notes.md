# P1823 proposal figure

Outputs: `fig1_P1823_example_preview.pdf` and `.png`.
Regenerate with `python make_fig1_single_example.py --target P1823`.

The supplied `Shooby_AGN` SPHEREx CSV matches P1823 / J162039.13+430814.6 to 0.31 arcsec. The bundled redshift is 0.6204823. All 20 archival optical spectra (2002–2024) and the September 24, 2026 NGPS spectrum are plotted. Blue shades run through the archival epochs in date order; the legend groups them by year for readability. Orange shows NGPS using the same saved P330E standard choice as the A3/P2190 figures, without inter-epoch rescaling. Full dates and continuum summaries are in `inputs/P1823_optical_epoch_summary.csv`.

The four-panel layout retains the 15-arcsec SDSS gri cutout, 5-arcsec scale bar, ZTF and W1 histories, and separate optical and SPHEREx spectra in observed microns and F-nu mJy. The cutout comes from the existing 40-arcsec SDSS image embedded in the observer page. The compact optical panel covers 0.34–0.94 microns so the noisy far-red edge does not determine the vertical scale; all redshifted Mg II through [O III] diagnostics remain visible. The larger combined spectrum retains the complete supplied optical wavelength arrays. NGPS is smoothed at 6 Angstrom FWHM within native valid segments for display, with masked pixels and arm breaks preserved. Archival spectra are plotted as stored in the observer-page snapshot, including its original sampling.

SPHEREx uses all 373 numerical measurements, their flux uncertainties and spectral half-widths. Observing groups are July–August 2025 (142 points), January–February 2026 (135), and July–August 2026 (96). The SPHEREx panels use a 0–2.2 mJy display range to emphasize the continuum slope. The isolated 3.05 mJy point near 4.1 microns is retained in the data and represented by an upward arrow labelled with its measured flux. All other measurements remain visible. The compact-panel legend is above the axes to avoid the long-wavelength continuum. This native-image CSV has a different metadata schema from the earlier A3/P2190 files; the loader verifies individual native images and preserves its exported variance errors and raw quality-flag summaries. Line labels are expected positions at the measured redshift, not fitted detections.

The 2026 spectrum has the highest median continuum in each sampled rest-frame window: 3000–3100, 4200–4250 and 5100–5200 Angstrom. It is approximately 3%, 3% and 8% above the brightest archival median in those respective windows. These descriptive differences do not include inter-epoch absolute calibration/slit-loss uncertainty and do not establish a statistically distinct new maximum. The optical photometry supports an overall recent rise with fluctuations. WISE shows an earlier decline followed by recovery, so “sustained recent brightening” is more accurate than “ever increasing.” No SPHEREx variability significance has been inferred from this figure.
