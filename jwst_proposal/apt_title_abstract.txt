REVIEW DRAFT — 24 targets, 12 fading and 12 rising; MIRI/MRS only; Medium program, 67.0 h from APT with reviewed sky fields and exposure/acquisition checks.

Title:
Does an AGN's infrared spectrum remember its accretion history?

Abstract:
An AGN can fade while its warm dust still responds to an earlier bright state; after brightening, the dust can lag behind the new nuclear power. We request 67.0 hours of MIRI/MRS spectroscopy of 24 AGN at z<0.3, divided into 12 fading and 12 rising sources, to measure the amplitude and persistence of this spectral imprint and test its physical origin. Every target has ZTF/WISE light curves, SPHEREx spectra and multi-epoch optical spectroscopy, including 2026 Palomar/NGPS spectra. These records constrain the nuclear history and hot-dust response. MIRI adds the warm continuum, both silicate features and spatial constraints on host emission. Our primary measurement is the nuclear 8-13 micron continuum excess or deficit relative to equilibrium at current power, and its dependence on change direction, amplitude and event age. We will account for luminosity, geometry, extinction and host light, then test whether delayed heating of fixed dust suffices or whether the dust distribution must evolve. One MIRI epoch per target samples different response stages across the ensemble. The programme will measure, or bound, the bias from recent variability when equilibrium infrared templates are used to infer nuclear power and dust covering factor in strongly variable AGN.
