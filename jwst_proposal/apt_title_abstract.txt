Title:
Does circumnuclear dust remember an AGN's accretion history?

Abstract:
An AGN can change its accretion state within years, but does its surrounding dust simply cool or heat, or does the dust distribution itself change? Changing-look AGN provide a direct experiment because their luminosity transitions can be dated. We propose MIRI/MRS spectroscopy of six AGN at z = 0.11–0.28: a faded nucleus, a recovering nucleus, a brightening nucleus, and three comparisons with less dramatic optical histories. Multi-epoch optical spectroscopy and ZTF/WISE light curves establish their histories; newly extracted SPHEREx spectra of the brightening example trace the rising infrared continuum. The missing measurement is the connection to warm dust. MIRI will obtain the 5–28 micron continuum, both silicate features, and the aromatic bands and ionic lines needed to constrain host contamination. We will test whether a fixed dust distribution, illuminated by an uncertain but observationally constrained luminosity history with light-travel delays, can reproduce these measurements together. A robust additional or depleted hot component would require a changing inner emitting distribution; agreement would bound restructuring and establish how much apparent dust evolution can arise from delayed illumination. The experiment turns existing time series into a physical test of whether infrared emission records the current accretion state or its history.
