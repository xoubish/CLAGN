Title:
Does an AGN's infrared spectrum remember its accretion history?

Abstract:
An AGN can fade while its warm dust still responds to an earlier bright state; after brightening, the dust can lag behind the new nuclear power. How long does this infrared memory persist, and does dust replenish after the nucleus fades? We request 67.0 hours of MIRI/MRS spectroscopy of 24 AGN at z<0.3 with recorded rises, declines, flares and recoveries. ZTF/WISE monitoring, SPHEREx measurements and multi-epoch optical spectroscopy connect nuclear activity to hot dust. MIRI supplies the warm continuum, both silicate profiles, and PAH and spatial diagnostics that constrain host emission. We will jointly fit the spectra and activity histories, anchored by 2010 W3/W4 photometry, to test whether warm spectra depend on prior illumination at comparable current nuclear power. Delayed heating and cooling of a fixed dust distribution is the null model. Fading and recovering sources test whether dust replenishment or redistribution is required, with rising sources providing the comparison. One MIRI visit per target constrains response models across the ensemble. The results will bound permitted dust evolution and establish when recent activity causes mid-infrared emission to misrepresent the current nucleus, accounting for uncertainties in illumination, geometry, host light and calibration.
