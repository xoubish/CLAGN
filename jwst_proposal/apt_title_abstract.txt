Title:
Does circumnuclear dust remember an AGN's accretion history?

Abstract:
Large luminosity changes in quasars and other active galactic nuclei provide timed experiments on circumnuclear dust. We ask whether delayed illumination of a fixed dust distribution explains their infrared spectra or whether the emitting distribution must evolve. We propose joint MIRI/MRS and NIRSpec IFU spectroscopy of an 11-object working sample of variable nuclei, including catalogue changing-look AGN. Selection prioritizes the time since a luminosity change relative to the expected dust delay, the duration and amplitude of that change, and the coverage of the preceding history. Broad-line appearance or disappearance is not required. Rising, sustained faint and recovering histories provide complementary tests; the final sample must establish coverage of these response phases at the allowed JWST dates. SPHEREx already covers 0.75–5 microns. MIRI adds the longer-wavelength warm continuum, accessible silicate features and host diagnostics; NIRSpec adds nuclear line and continuum detail near the MIRI epoch. P1823 illustrates the measured baseline through its high optical state, earlier WISE decline and recovery, and three SPHEREx epochs. One epoch per JWST instrument is fitted jointly with the earlier optical, WISE and available SPHEREx data at their actual dates. Both fixed and evolving dust models include light-travel delays, extinction and host light. The result will be evidence or limits on how dust adjusts to changing accretion luminosity.
