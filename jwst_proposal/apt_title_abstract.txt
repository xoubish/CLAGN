Title:
Does an AGN's infrared spectrum remember its accretion history?

Abstract:
An AGN can fade while its warm dust still responds to an earlier bright state; after brightening, the dust can lag behind the new nuclear power. We request 67.0 hours of MIRI/MRS spectroscopy of 24 AGN at z<0.3 with recorded rises, declines, flares and recoveries. ZTF/WISE histories, SPHEREx coverage and optical spectroscopy, including 2026 Palomar/NGPS observations, connect nuclear activity to hot dust. MIRI adds three complementary constraints: the warm continuum, both silicate profiles, and PAH emission and spatial structure that constrain the host. We will jointly fit these spectra and the recorded histories to test how the warm response depends on earlier illumination at comparable current nuclear power. The 2010 W3/W4 measurements supply an additional historical anchor. Delayed heating of fixed dust is the null model; evidence for evolution requires continuum and silicate discrepancies robust to allowed geometry, illumination, extinction and host contributions, with an improved fit from an evolving distribution. Sources compatible with both families constrain the permitted response. One MIRI epoch per target provides the warm spectral information needed for this ensemble test and for assessing variability-induced biases in infrared estimates of nuclear power and dust covering factor.
