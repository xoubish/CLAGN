Title:
Does circumnuclear dust remember an AGN's accretion history?

Abstract:
Changing-look active galactic nuclei change their accretion luminosity by factors of several within a few years, and their infrared light curves show that the surrounding dust responds. What is not known is whether the dust merely re-adjusts its temperature to a changed illumination, or whether the inner dust structure itself is destroyed and re-formed as the sublimation radius moves. We propose MIRI/MRS spectroscopy of six variability-selected AGN at z = 0.11–0.28 with dated luminosity histories: two nuclei that faded by factors of 5–10 in 2019 and 2014–19 (one now recovering), one that has brightened by a factor of two since 2021, and three comparison AGN with steady histories at similar luminosity and redshift. Each has multi-epoch optical spectroscopy from 2014–2026 including a September 2026 epoch, eight-year ZTF and fourteen-year WISE light curves, and 2010 WISE 12 and 22 micron photometry from before the transitions. We forward-model the measured illumination histories with light-travel delays and radiative equilibrium, and test whether a fixed dust distribution reproduces the 5–28 micron continuum and the 9.7 and 18 micron silicate features; re-formed inner dust predicts a hotter near-infrared bump and a 5–25 percent excess at 5–8 microns, while a change in line-of-sight obscuration predicts silicate absorption. The mid-infrared monitoring of these sources ended with NEOWISE in 2024; JWST is the only way to measure their dust spectra in 2027. The program needs 14 hours of charged time.
