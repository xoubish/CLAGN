\scientificjustification

\noindent\textbf{Does the dust around a changing nucleus keep up with it?}
An active galactic nucleus (AGN) can undergo large luminosity changes within years [1,2], sometimes accompanied by a transformation of its continuum and broad-line spectrum [3--5]. Its surrounding dust records these changes through delayed heating and through destruction or replenishment of the emitting material. Individual nuclei show that dust boundaries evolve over years [6,7]; the missing step is a sample that reveals how the response depends on the direction and history of nuclear activity. Changing-look and strongly variable AGN followed through rises, declines, flares and recoveries provide exactly this experiment.

We request \textbf{67.0~h of MIRI/MRS spectroscopy of 24 AGN at $z<0.3$} with recorded rises, declines, flares and recoveries. ZTF/WISE monitoring, SPHEREx coverage and archival plus 2026 optical spectroscopy connect nuclear activity to the hot-dust response. Together they catch dust at different stages of its response. Only MIRI now covers the warm continuum and both silicate features, with the spatial information that separates nucleus from host, tying each history to its warm-dust spectrum. The programme addresses two linked questions:

\noindent\textbf{1. How long does warm-dust memory persist?} Delayed heating can produce a warm excess after a decline and a deficit after a rise [8,9]. The recorded histories and 2010 WISE measurements constrain response models and test whether this spectral memory persists at the MIRI epoch.

\noindent\textbf{2. Does dust replenish after the nucleus fades?} Delayed heating and cooling of unchanged dust is the null model. The silicate features and 5--28~$\mu$m continuum test whether fading and recovering sources require an altered dust distribution [6--9], with rising sources providing the comparison.

\noindent\textbf{From infrared echoes to dust evolution.}
WISE observations have established infrared echoes of changing-look transitions [10--13], supporting intrinsic changes in nuclear power in those sources. Broadband colours, however, combine temperature, emitting area, extinction and host light [14--16]. The proposed spectra separate these contributions, measuring the warm signature of each history and constraining which properties of the emitting material produce it.

The equilibrium sublimation radius scales approximately as $L^{1/2}$ [17--19], but the physical dust boundary can adjust over years [6]. Individual nuclei already suggest different responses: the smaller dust radius after Mrk~590 faded supports replenishment [7], whereas the temperature variations in Mrk~817 favour heating and cooling at a nearly fixed boundary [20]. Fading and recovering sources therefore target the uncertain replenishment of dust, with rising sources testing the response to increased illumination. The comparison asks whether the spectrum depends on the path to its present state.

\noindent\textbf{MIRI's diagnostic power.}
SPHEREx covers 0.75--5~$\mu$m [21], constraining hot dust close to the nucleus [20,22]. Longer delays and weaker variability at longer wavelengths [8,23,24] make the warm continuum sensitive to earlier illumination. MRS supplies the essential 4.9--27.9~$\mu$m spectrum [25]: the continuum measures the imprint, the paired silicate features constrain geometry and optical depth [26,27], and spatial profiles and aromatic bands constrain host emission [16,28,29]. MIRI extends the NEOWISE/SPHEREx record into the warm-dust regime and connects it to the 2010 W3/W4 baseline. This combination tests how dust at different temperatures responds to the same recorded activity.

\noindent\textbf{A sample selected to test dust memory.}
Our targets span sustained rises, declines, flares, plateaus and recoveries, testing the direction, amplitude and timing of the dust response. Recovering sources ask whether nuclei at comparable current power retain different warm spectra because they followed different paths. We combine catalogued changing-look AGN [30--33] with large-amplitude ZTF/WISE variables [5,34], selecting across spectral classifications [31,35]. A variability manifold identifies candidates with similar light-curve shapes [36]; available multi-epoch spectra establish continuum and broad-line changes. Visual inspection shows no obvious mergers or very close projected companions. Each target has approximately a decade of W1/W2 monitoring, with 95th-to-5th-percentile flux ratios of 1.3--2.3 before host subtraction. SPHEREx measurements supplement these histories, with repeated all-sky passes adding near-infrared epochs before the MIRI observations.

\noindent\textbf{From dust components to their response.}
Published observations demonstrate the three elements of our experiment: separating hot and warm dust (Figure~\ref{fig:diagnostics}A), constraining central and surrounding emission (B), and interpreting a delayed infrared response using the activity history (C). The rest-frame 8--13~$\mu$m dust luminosity includes broad silicate emission or absorption, with PAHs and narrow lines fitted separately. Continuum slopes and paired silicate profiles constrain the temperature distribution, optical depth and geometry, including when an integrated flux is consistent with its historical value.

\noindent\textbf{Connecting spectral shape to the recorded history.}
We will jointly fit the warm spectrum, current nuclear power and each source's full light curve, including reversals. Optical decomposition, WISE and SPHEREx constrain illumination and hot dust at their observed epochs [4,20,22]; the optical photometry nearest each JWST visit constrains the current state, with temporal gaps propagated as uncertainty. Figure~\ref{fig:diagnostics}C illustrates why earlier illumination matters: the warm response can be delayed and broadened. Each target contributes one warm spectral snapshot; the spread of event ages across the sample replaces repeat visits. The 2010 W3/W4 measurements provide additional constraints through their bandpasses and a shared host model. Event dates, gaps and the optical-to-heating conversion enter as uncertainties; an old flare without a warm imprint itself bounds the memory timescale.

\noindent\textbf{Turning histories into a test of dust memory.}
For each target, we will compare its current nuclear power with its earlier illumination, averaged over the delays allowed by the dust-response models. Sources brighter in the past may retain excess warm emission, whereas recently brightened sources may show an incomplete warm response. Flares and recoveries test whether this dependence persists beyond a simple rising/fading classification. We will test whether the recorded histories explain spectral differences that current power alone cannot, propagating uncertainties in host emission, geometry and unobserved activity.

\noindent\textbf{Measurement precision and interpretation.}
An illustrative shell-model calculation for the rising source in Figure~\ref{fig:history} (bottom), using dust opacities from [37], gives conditional $1\sigma$ uncertainties near 5\% on the extracted 8--13~$\mu$m flux and 0.08/0.04 on the 9.7/18~$\mu$m contrasts, defined as $S_\lambda=\ln(F_\nu/F_{\nu,\mathrm{cont}})$ relative to a local power-law continuum. These estimates assume the requested S/N, correlated calibration errors of 3\% in scale and tilt, and a smooth host residual of 10\% at rest 12~$\mu$m. At this precision a single object does not separate the two families; the discrimination comes from the ensemble, where 24 histories with different event ages and directions must be reproduced by one response model. Figure~\ref{fig:diagnostics} shows the observed diagnostics on which the joint analysis rests.

\noindent\textbf{Testing whether delayed heating is sufficient.}
Clumpy and disc-plus-wind geometries [38--40] define the fixed-distribution null: dust changes temperature in response to the reconstructed history [8,9]. An accretion-fed dusty wind [41] motivates an alternative in which the dust supply and covering factor can change. We will test evolving distributions with dust destruction or replenishment [6,7,22], including light-travel delays and composition and grain-size uncertainty [42] in both families; a preference for evolution would not uniquely identify a wind. We summarize the warm residual by $\delta_{\rm warm}=\log_{10}[L^{\rm MRS}_{8\text{--}13\,\mu{\rm m}}/L^{\rm fixed}_{8\text{--}13\,\mu{\rm m}}(t_{\rm MIRI})]$, relative to the \emph{delayed fixed-distribution} prediction. Evidence for evolution requires joint continuum and silicate discrepancies across the allowed fixed models [28], robust to host and calibration uncertainties [43], and an improved fit from an evolving distribution. Where both families fit, the data still bound the warm response and the permitted evolution. Across the recorded event ages, these model-dependent constraints test allowed replenishment timescales, the radial extent of responding dust, and how long mid-infrared emission can misrepresent a changed nucleus [44,45].

\noindent\textbf{Complementary tests of obscuration and excitation.}
Paired silicate profiles, optical histories and high-ionization lines constrain obscuration and excitation [26,27]. [Ne\,\textsc{vi}] 7.7 and [Ne\,\textsc{v}] 14.3~$\mu$m are accessible throughout the sample [46]; the nominal MRS range includes [Ne\,\textsc{v}] 24.3~$\mu$m for 13 targets and [O\,\textsc{iv}] 25.9~$\mu$m for nine. Line detections or limits provide supporting diagnostics; they are not instantaneous measures of nuclear power.

\noindent\textbf{Central and surrounding dust.}
Here, surrounding emission denotes the spatially resolved host component after PSF modelling (see Description of Observations). Where detected, we compare it with central emission at matched resolution within the common field. PAH 11.3/7.7~$\mu$m ratios, continuum slopes and silicate strengths probe dust processing, heating and obscuration [29,47]. The decomposition in Figure~\ref{fig:diagnostics}B demonstrates how surrounding emission can be isolated to constrain host templates [28], testing whether a central warm excess survives allowed host contributions. This measures the resolved dust environment around the unresolved variable core.

\descriptionofobservations

\noindent\textbf{Spectral coverage and sensitivity.}
Each target receives one MIRI/MRS science visit and a matching sky visit, using all three grating settings, nuclear acquisition and four-point dithers [25,48]. The 4.9--27.9~$\mu$m coverage includes both silicate peaks for all 24 targets; the primary rest-frame 8--13~$\mu$m diagnostic does not require channel~4C.

Archival total-source W3/W4 fluxes span 1.9--48.4/6.4--82.6~mJy. We target S/N of 50 per $R=100$ bin near rest 5 and 12~$\mu$m and 30 at 18~$\mu$m (2\%/3.3\% statistical errors). APT specifies the FULL/FASTR1 integrations, with extra setting-B exposure for the W3-faint J083826.50+371906.7. Equal-depth sky exposures preserve extended host emission [48]. Science-plus-sky integration totals 17.00~h; \textbf{the 67.0~h request} includes acquisition and all APT~2026.5.1 overheads, making this a Medium programme [49].

For J083826.50+371906.7 at $z=0.2111$, ETC/Pandeia~2026.7 predicts S/N of 55/225/61 per $R=100$ bin at rest 5/12/18~$\mu$m. These estimates assume a point source with $F_\nu\propto\lambda^2$, 0.95~mJy in W3 (half the archival total), the adopted sky spectrum, equal-depth sky exposures and extraction radii of $0.3''$/$0.6''$/$0.9''$. They establish statistical sensitivity under the adopted nuclear-flux model; host and calibration uncertainties enter the science inference separately. Acquisition uses F560W for the faintest sources and the neutral-density filter for brighter nuclei, with readouts giving S/N above 50 without saturation over the adopted flux ranges [50].

Available optical photometry constrains the JWST-epoch state with allowance for temporal gaps; WISE/SPHEREx histories and 2010 W3/W4 photometry constrain earlier states.

\noindent\textbf{Separating nuclear dust from the host.}
\textbf{Each MRS cube measures the nucleus and surrounding host together.} The PSF FWHM grows from approximately $0.3''$ at 5~$\mu$m to $1''$ at 25~$\mu$m, sampling roughly 0.1--4.5~kpc across the sample; channel fields span $3.2''\!\times3.7''$ to $6.6''\!\times7.7''$ [25]. Wavelength-dependent PSF fitting [51] separates unresolved and resolved emission [28]. Surrounding spectra constrain host templates with allowance for spatial variation. Joint modelling separates continuum, silicate, PAH and H$_2$ contributions [15,47]; PAHs allow for AGN processing [29], while [Ne\,\textsc{v}] and [Ne\,\textsc{vi}] trace highly ionised gas [46]. Alternative extractions and host templates [16] bound the nuclear flux, including compact host light within the unresolved component.

We will integrate observed-frame MRS spectra through the WISE responses, using the measured spectral shape to calculate synthetic W3/W4 fluxes in the WISE calibration convention. Comparison with 2010 requires matching the WISE spatial response: constant host light cancels in a flux difference only for the same spatial contribution. WISE imaging and aperture photometry constrain emission beyond the MRS field; optical and 2MASS profiles guide stellar-host morphology. Models account for spectral coverage gaps and host light within the SPHEREx aperture. We propagate nuclear-flux bounds from alternative extractions and host templates, uncertainty in the aperture matching, W3/W4 calibration errors and wavelength-dependent MRS calibration covariance [43].

\supplementalinformation
\noindent\textbf{Special requirements.} Each science/sky pair is linked as a consecutive, noninterruptible sequence to obtain a contemporaneous background [48]. Sky fields are selected using WISE and 2MASS catalogues and images, with at least $25''$ clearance from catalogued sources. Twenty-three fields lie within 1--2~arcmin of their nuclei; the crowded field around J20145928+2523010 uses a $135''$ offset. Background targets are marked extended and use four-point extended-source dithers, with no target acquisition. No fixed-date constraints, coordinated parallels or joint-facility time are requested. APT's Visit Planner returns scheduling windows for all 48 visits.

\noindent\textbf{Duplications.} A coordinate-based MAST search on 2026 September 26, including archived and planned JWST observations, found no overlapping observations within $120''$ of any of the 24 science positions. No potential MIRI/MRS duplications were identified.
